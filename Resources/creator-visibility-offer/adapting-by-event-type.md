# Adapting the Offer by Event Type

The three layers (talent access, creator management, visibility report) stay
the same for every client. What changes is who buys, which creators fit,
what the client counts as success, and what the sponsor value section
measures.

Use this page to rewrite the deck and the sample report for a new client.
Swap the rows below into the "Why now", "Where we stand" and "What the client
receives" slides.

## Quick reference

| Event type | Who buys | What they need to prove | Creators that fit | Extra report metrics |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sports league or fight night | Head of marketing, commercial lead | Audience size and growth for sponsors and investors | Fight, fitness, lifestyle, food creators; streamers | Concurrent stream viewers, walkout and finish moments, sponsor screen time |
| Music festival | Promoter, partnerships lead | Reach of the festival brand beyond ticket holders; sponsor activation value | Music, fashion, lifestyle creators; DJs; streamers | Sponsor booth visits shown on camera, artist moment reach, next year ticket intent |
| Concert or tour date | Promoter, artist management | Buzz in each city, ticket sell through | Music and culture creators, fan accounts | Ticket link clicks per creator, city by city reach |
| Conference or trade fair | Organiser, government body | International visibility, delegate attraction for next edition | Business, tech, travel creators; journalists | Speaker clip reach, country reach, exhibitor mentions |
| Tourism campaign | Ministry, tourism board | Global awareness of the destination | Travel, food, culture creators; global talent | Diaspora reach, international country split, search or booking signals where supplied |
| Brand launch or activation | Brand marketing team | Product visibility and conversions | Category creators matched to the brand | Promo code redemptions, product on screen time |
| Cultural event or carnival | Organiser, city, ministry of culture | Cultural export, tourism pull | Culture, dance, food, travel creators | International audience share, diaspora reach |

## What to change in each part

### Talent access

- Pick creators by the audience the client needs to reach, not by follower
  count alone. A festival wants music and lifestyle; a trade fair wants
  business and travel.
- Always include one creator from each target market. The combat sports
  sample report recommendation was a creator in the second biggest market,
  because that market delivered views with no local creator.
- Streamers fit anything with a live moment: fights, headline sets, award
  nights, opening ceremonies.

### Creator management

- Rewrite the brief around the client's call to action: tickets, replay,
  next edition registration, booking a trip, buying a product.
- Map the moments worth filming: walkout and finish for fights; headline set
  and crowd for festivals; keynote and networking for conferences.
- Agree where sponsor branding should appear in shot.

### The report

Keep the structure in `report-and-data.md`. Change:

- **The title and event details.**
- **The sponsor value section.** What counts as a sponsor moment differs:
  a branded cage, a festival stage banner, a conference backdrop.
- **The audience section.** Tourism and cultural clients care most about the
  international and diaspora split. Leagues care about home market depth and
  regional spread.
- **The action section.** Use the client's own conversion: tickets,
  registrations, bookings, codes.

### The deck

The eight slide structure works for every client:

1. Cover: AToure × client logo, "Building the foundation for maximising
   [client] visibility".
2. Where we stand: what is already working (researched, sourced) and what
   the next stage needs.
3. Why now: the client's growth story plus the revenue model panel.
4. The AToure answer: two deliverables, managed creators and the report.
5. Inside the report: what the client receives, with a sample image.
6. The proposal: a trial at one event.
7. Track record.
8. The ask, with contact details.

Slide 2 is the one to rewrite with care. Use public facts with sources, say
in the speaker notes that they come from public reporting, and frame the
right side as what the next stage needs, not as faults.
