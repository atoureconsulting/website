Our Professional Opinion

We Recommend Pushing
the Start Date to July 12

We want to be direct with you. The original June 10 start is not in the best interest of the tour, Ashton's brand, or Atouré's. Here are five compounding reasons why.

01
Lead Time Is Not Enough
Twelve days is not enough time to build what this tour deserves. Atouré's value is in the infrastructure engineered before the team lands — state permits, government relationships, security choreography, cultural moments conceived and prepared. IShowSpeed's results happened because every element was in place before he stepped off the plane. With 12 days we build blind. We can execute a functional tour — but we want to be in a position to execute an exceptional one. An exceptional one is the only kind worth Ashton's name or ours.
02
Yellow Fever — The Team Cannot Travel on June 10
Entry into Ivory Coast, Benin, Ghana, and Senegal requires a valid International Certificate of Yellow Fever Vaccination. This is a mandatory legal requirement — without it, members of the delegation will be refused entry at the border. The vaccine takes 10 days to reach effectiveness after administration. For the team to be cleared for June 10, every member would need to have been vaccinated by May 31 — two days from now. This alone makes June 10 an unrealistic and potentially dangerous departure date. A July 12 start gives the full team adequate time to vaccinate and receive their yellow cards.
03
Peak Monsoon Season Across West Africa
June is the heaviest rainfall month across Ghana, Ivory Coast, Benin, and Senegal. This directly impacts helicopter operations, outdoor filming locations, road and convoy conditions, and the visual quality of every piece of content. The tour is designed to show Africa at its most compelling — its beauty, its contrast, its energy. Heavy skies and flooded streets work against that entirely. The monsoon begins easing from late July. By August, conditions across the region are significantly improved. Touring in June risks producing content that does not represent Africa — or Ashton — at their best.
04
The World Cup Has Absorbed the Market
The FIFA World Cup 2026 runs until July 19. Every country on this tour — Morocco, Senegal, Ivory Coast, Ghana — has a national team in the tournament. Beyond the audience attention being pulled toward the World Cup, the deeper issue is operational: the brands, agencies, security firms, production companies, local fixers, and venues that Atouré relies on have invested heavily — both financially and in manpower — into World Cup activations across these markets through mid-July. The resources we would normally activate are currently deployed elsewhere. Starting June 10 means building a world-class tour on a depleted infrastructure.
05
This Reflects on Atouré
We built our reputation on executing at the highest level. We would rather be honest now and deliver something world-class than rush and produce something average. The IShowSpeed numbers did not happen by accident — they happened because the groundwork was right. We want those numbers for Ashton, and we believe this tour can exceed them. But only if we do it properly.

Operational Recommendation

Team Size Matters

At this stage — with late hotel bookings, multi-country logistics, and tight scheduling — group size is one of the most important cost and quality levers on the entire tour.

10
Ideal

The sweet spot. Streamlined, fast-moving, and cost-efficient without sacrificing production quality.

  • Hotel bookings at this stage — flexible
  • Lower per-head visa cost
  • Faster airport clearance
  • More authentic content — smaller crew = less staged
  • Lower security footprint needed
15
Maximum

Manageable but requires tighter coordination. Do not exceed this number at this stage of planning.

  • Approx 4 vehicles for the team
  • Hotel room block required — limited availability
  • Higher security requirement
  • More complex visa management
  • Increased per-country operational cost
20+
Avoid

At this late stage of planning, a group of 20+ creates significant operational, financial, and content problems.

  • Hotel availability at this late stage — critical risk
  • Charter flight cost increases substantially
  • Security complexity spikes
  • Clunky on the ground — slows every movement
  • Content suffers — too much logistics noise
  • Budget blowout across every category
