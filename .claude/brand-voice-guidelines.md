## Executive Summary

AToure Management & Consulting operates, it does not advertise. Every piece of brand voice guidance below traces back to one fact: AToure builds the project and runs it on the ground as the operator, rather than selling a campaign and handing delivery to someone else. The voice has to sound like that is true, not merely claim it.

Two distinct voices share this brand: the company page (AToure, institutional, proof led, never first person) and Baba Toure's personal profile (Director, first person, operator stories and market opinion). They share the same facts and the same hard rules, but differ in person, structure, and job. The company voice exists to generate leads from brands, investors, and federations evaluating AToure's credibility. Baba's voice exists to build his own standing in the market access and creator economy space, using the same proof points from a personal, on the ground angle.

The single most distinctive mechanical trait of this brand's voice: no hyphens, no dashes, anywhere, ever, in anything written for AToure. This is not a style preference, it is an absolute constraint that has been enforced repeatedly and without exception across every piece of copy produced this session.

## We Are / We Are Not

| We Are | We Are Not |
|--------|------------|
| **An operator** — we build the project and run it ourselves, on the ground, from the first meeting to the last day | **An agency** — never call AToure one, in any language, in any document, even in a comparison ("we're not like traditional agencies") |
| **Proof led** — every claim traces to a specific, verified project (AFCON, İshowSpeed, Ashton Hall's Africa Tour, DAZN Qatar, Race Weekend) | **Vague or aspirational** — no claims about capability in the abstract, no "we can help you" without a project behind it |
| **Specific with numbers, and honest about their limits** — figures are stated plainly, then undercut with the human result | **Inflated or borrowed** — never use a bigger adjacent number (a tournament's total audience, a platform's aggregate reach) as if it were AToure's own attributable result |
| **Institutional and relationship based** — access comes from government, federation, and partner relationships built over years | **Transactional or shortcut seeking** — the brand never implies a deal can be bought or rushed past the relationship stage |
| **Director and Founder** — Baba Toure holds both, and both are valid titles | **Just a title holder** — he operates inside the work personally, not from a distance |
| **Plain spoken** — short declarative fragments, full stops and commas over complex punctuation, no jargon, no "excited to share" thought leader voice | **Corporate or performed** — no hedging, no buzzwords, no borrowed LinkedIn thought leadership cadence |
| **Operates projects, not people** — AToure manages projects and delivery; it facilitates talent and creator relationships | **A talent management or representation shop** — never describe AToure as managing, representing, or being the agency for a creator |

### Voice Attributes Detail

#### Operator, not reseller
- **What it means**: AToure is paid to deliver an outcome itself, with its own team, not to broker a deal and step aside.
- **How it shows up**: Copy describes AToure's own team doing the work ("we run it on the ground," "AToure managed İshowSpeed's tour") rather than AToure arranging for someone else to do it.
- **What to avoid**: Any phrasing that implies AToure sells a plan and hands execution to a third party, or acts as a pass through between a brand and local vendors.
- **Evidence**: CLAUDE.md: "it builds the project and runs it on the ground as the operator, rather than selling a campaign and handing the delivery to someone else." Strategy doc positioning section: "This is the line that separates AToure from every agency pitch a brand has already heard."
- **Confidence**: High (stated directly, multiple times, by the user)

#### Never an agency, in word or in framing
- **What it means**: The word "agency" cannot be used to describe AToure, including in a negated comparison.
- **How it shows up**: Company About copy was corrected live in this session to remove "We are an agency. And we are much more." A later batch of 20 posts had "traditional agency" and "Some agencies will tell you..." removed at the user's instruction even though those uses were negating the comparison, not claiming it.
- **What to avoid**: "We're not like other agencies," "traditional agency," "PR agency" when describing AToure's own category. A third party being called an agency (a "collaborating agency" the brand might also be working with) is acceptable, since it does not describe AToure itself.
- **Evidence**: CLAUDE.md: "AToure is not an agency. Never call it one, in any language, in any document." Confirmed again live: user flagged "agency" language in two drafted posts and asked for it removed even though AToure was the one saying it was not an agency.
- **Confidence**: High

#### Manages projects, facilitates talent
- **What it means**: "Manage" is accurate when the object is a project, tour, or delivery. It is not accurate when the object is a creator or public figure, since that implies agency style representation.
- **How it shows up**: "AToure managed İshowSpeed's tour through Morocco and Ivory Coast" is correct. "AToure manages high profile public figures" was corrected to "facilitates" during this session.
- **What to avoid**: "Talent management" as a service description; any phrasing that casts a creator as AToure's client or roster rather than a partner in a project AToure runs.
- **Evidence**: User, live correction: "we dont manage creators, we are not an agency" followed later by the clarifying refinement: "we dont manage creators but we do manage projects. so its fine to say we managed the ishowspeed tour."
- **Confidence**: High (direct user statement, including the specific exception)

#### Figures are stated plainly, then undercut
- **What it means**: A number is never left to do the persuading alone. House style states a result, then immediately grounds it in a human outcome.
- **How it shows up**: "But the numbers never tell the full story," followed by the human result, is the client's own established line.
- **What to avoid**: Closing a post or section on a raw statistic with no follow through sentence.
- **Evidence**: CLAUDE.md: "The numbers get stated plainly, then undercut. The client's own line is 'But the numbers never tell the full story,' followed by the human result."
- **Confidence**: High

#### Only verified, attributable figures
- **What it means**: A number only appears if it has been explicitly confirmed, and only if it is actually AToure's own attributable result rather than a larger adjacent figure.
- **How it shows up**: The AFCON tournament's total figures (6.1 billion impressions, 5.2 billion video views) were identified mid session as not AToure's own number, since they measure the whole tournament, not İshowSpeed's specific contribution. The correct, confirmed, attributable figure (16 million stream views plus 500 million clip views, 516 million total) replaced them everywhere.
- **What to avoid**: Reaching for the most impressive number in the source material without checking whether it is actually attributable to AToure's own work. When in doubt, ask rather than estimate.
- **Evidence**: CLAUDE.md: "Do not publish a view count that has not been supplied or verified... Ask rather than estimate." Confirmed live when the user corrected the 6.1/5.2 billion figures: "the 6.1 and 5.2 is regarding afcons total views and speed contributed to that. not only speed."
- **Confidence**: High

## Brand Personality

- **Archetype**: The operator who has already done it. Not a strategist who theorizes about African markets from outside them, someone who has sat in the room, built the relationships, and can point to the delivered project.
- **If our brand were a person**: Direct, unhurried, allergic to overclaiming. Would rather undersell a result and let the specifics do the convincing than reach for a big adjective.
- **Core values expressed in voice**: Earned access over bought access. Patience with institutional process over speed for its own sake. Plain language over performance.

## Messaging Framework

### Primary Value Proposition
AToure delivers market access across Africa for brands, companies, investors, creators and private clients, then runs the work itself on the ground, rather than selling a plan and handing delivery to someone else.

Variations observed:
- "AToure Management & Consulting delivers market access across Africa for brands, companies, investors, creators and private clients, then runs the work on the ground." (Strategy doc, finalized company About)
- "AToure opens African markets for brands, companies, investors and private clients, then runs the work on the ground." (user's own working phrasing, Twenty Post Calendar source material)
- "I started AToure because I kept seeing the same gap: brands, investors, creators wanted into Africa, and nobody they could call already had people on the ground there." (Baba's personal About, first person variant)

### Key Message Pillars

1. **Market access proof (company voice)**
   - Core idea: show a completed project as evidence, not a capability claim.
   - When to use: company page posts, one case study per post.
   - Example phrasing: "For DAZN in Qatar, that meant AToure on the ground from the first conversation through to delivery. Same team throughout."
   - Frequency: core pillar, roughly half of company page content per the doc's content pillar split.

2. **The keys to Africa (company voice)**
   - Core idea: explain what makes operating in these markets different from entering cold, independent of any single case study.
   - When to use: standalone explainer posts, positioning content, About copy.
   - Example phrasing: "We only claim markets we have already worked in... We are the first step for these entities entering Africa."
   - Frequency: core pillar, the other half of company page content.

3. **Operator stories (Baba's voice)**
   - Core idea: a first person account of a specific moment that did not make the case study page.
   - When to use: Baba's personal posts, roughly alternating with market point of view posts.
   - Example phrasing: "Before AToure went into Ivory Coast, we had no existing relationships there. No local network. No precedent."
   - Frequency: half of Baba's content per the doc's pillar split.

4. **Market point of view (Baba's voice)**
   - Core idea: a plain, one idea per post opinion on Africa market access or talent facilitation as a field.
   - When to use: Baba's personal posts, the other half of his content.
   - Example phrasing: "In African markets, the relationship IS the contract."
   - Frequency: half of Baba's content.

### Competitive Positioning
- vs. a traditional agency: AToure runs the work itself with its own team; an agency sells a plan and hands delivery to someone else.
- vs. a logistics vendor: logistics is a small fraction of what market access actually requires (government relationships, visas, security, local partners); AToure provides the institutional access, not just the operational capability.
- vs. Status Quo (brands entering Africa without a partner): the brands that win are not the ones with the biggest budgets, they are the ones who went in with the right partner who understood the nuance before the contract was signed.

## Tone-by-Context Matrix

Voice is constant (operator, proof led, plain spoken, never an agency). Tone flexes by context.

| Context | Formality | Energy | Technical Depth | Key Principle |
|---------|-----------|--------|------------------|---------------|
| Company page, case study post | Medium High | Medium | Medium | Lead with the result, then the mechanism, close with the human outcome |
| Company page, positioning / explainer post | Medium High | Medium | Medium | State the structural claim directly, no hedging |
| Baba's personal post, operator story | Low Medium | Medium High | Low | First person, a specific moment, admit what went wrong or was uncertain |
| Baba's personal post, market opinion | Low Medium | High | Low | One idea per post, plain statement up front, short sentences |
| Website / case study page copy | High | Low Medium | Medium | Short declarative fragments, no verbs needed in capability lists |
| Official company descriptions (LinkedIn About, bios) | High | Low | Medium | Precise, every claim checkable, no filler |

### Context-Specific Guidelines

#### Company Page, Case Study Posts
- **Overall tone**: Confident but restrained. The project speaks for itself.
- **Opening approach**: A structural claim or a scene, not a question. ("A brand does not need to enter a new market to run a campaign there. It needs someone already standing in it.")
- **Do's**: Name the project, name AToure's own role in delivering it, close with the "numbers stated plainly, then undercut" pattern where a verified figure exists.
- **Don'ts**: Never use a tournament wide or platform wide aggregate figure as AToure's own number. Never call AToure an agency, even by contrast.
- **Example**: "For DAZN in Qatar, that meant AToure on the ground from the first conversation through to delivery. Same team throughout."

#### Baba's Personal Posts
- **Overall tone**: First person, direct, willing to admit a gap or a mistake.
- **Opening approach**: A specific scene or a plain, slightly contrarian statement ("In African markets, the relationship IS the contract").
- **Do's**: Use "I," reference a specific project he was personally involved in, end on a point of view rather than a pitch.
- **Don'ts**: Never pitch directly for business (that is the company page's job); never use "Founder" as his title (he is Director).
- **Example**: "I haven't posted here in a while. Not because there was nothing to say. There was a lot happening on the ground across seven African nations that never made it onto this page."

#### Official Descriptions (About sections, bios)
- **Overall tone**: High formality, zero filler, every sentence checkable.
- **Opening approach**: State what AToure does, in plain terms, in the first sentence.
- **Do's**: Name verified markets and verified work by name. Use "facilitation" for talent, "management" only for projects.
- **Don'ts**: No hyphens or dashes anywhere. No unverified figures. No "agency."
- **Example**: "AToure Management & Consulting delivers market access across Africa for brands, companies, investors, creators and private clients, then runs the work on the ground."

## Format Specifications

Settled directly by Baba Toure, covering the mechanics of how a post is built.

| Element | Rule |
|---------|------|
| IShowSpeed spelling | Always "IShowSpeed." No accent, not all caps. |

| Post length | Deliberately variable. Some posts are a few lines, others run the full story. Length is a choice made per post, not a fixed template. |
| Comment replies | Looser than the posts themselves. Conversational, casual replies to comments are fine and do not need to follow the full hyphen, dash, and terminology rules that apply to posts. |

## Language That Works

### Top Phrases
1. **"But the numbers never tell the full story."** — Context: immediately after stating a verified figure, Why it works: it is the client's own established line, and it signals confidence rather than insecurity about the number by choosing to look past it.
2. **"We only claim markets where we have done the work."** — Context: positioning and About copy, Why it works: converts a limitation (not operating everywhere) into a credibility signal.
3. **"[Scene or structural claim], not [the easier assumption]."** pattern, e.g. "Football in Africa isn't sport. It's infrastructure." — Context: post openers, Why it works: creates the small friction the user specifically asked hooks to generate, without resorting to a question or a list.

### Questions That Engage
Not applicable in the same way as a sales conversation: this brand's posts are built to open on a statement, not a question, per the established hook pattern (a counter intuitive claim or a scene, not a question).

## Language to Avoid

### Anti-Patterns
1. **"We are an agency. And we are much more."** — Problem: directly violates the never call it an agency rule, and was the single highest priority correction made to the live company page this session. Better: "AToure Management & Consulting is not an agency. We build the project and run it on the ground as the operator."
2. **Leading with an inflated or borrowed figure** (a tournament's total audience, a platform's aggregate reach) — Problem: overstates AToure's actual attributable result and risks being an unverifiable or misleading claim if challenged. Better: use the specific, confirmed, attributable number, even if smaller.
3. **"I'm excited to share..." / generic thought leader openers** — Problem: reads as performed rather than direct, and buries the hook past the two line cutoff LinkedIn applies before "see more." Better: open on the structural claim or scene itself.
4. **Hyphenated compounds and em dashes under time pressure** — Problem: easy to slip back into default writing habits (mobile-first, decision-making, a mid sentence em dash); this is the most consistently violated rule across every batch of drafted copy. Better: a deliberate final pass on every piece of copy checking specifically for hyphens and dashes before it ships.
